What Do You Need to Know About Plumbing?

Plumbing problems are not always dramatic at first. A small leak, loose connection, slow drain or running toilet can become a larger property problem if it is ignored.

This guide explains common plumbing issues property owners encounter, what warning signs to look for, what basic checks may be reasonable and when a plumbing problem should be handled by a professional.

Common Plumbing Problems

Plumbing Problems Property Owners Should Watch For

Some plumbing problems are obvious, while others begin as small changes that are easy to overlook. Common warning signs include dripping taps, damp cupboards, water around pipe connections, slow drains, unpleasant drain smells, toilets that do not stop filling, reduced water pressure and unexplained moisture.

The location of the water matters. Water under a sink may point towards a connection or fitting, while moisture on a wall or ceiling may require a different investigation. A visible symptom does not always identify the source of the problem.

Leaking Taps

A dripping tap may appear minor, but continuous water loss can indicate worn internal components, seals or other problems. If tightening or basic adjustment does not resolve the issue, the fitting may need repair or replacement.

Leaks Under Sinks

Water beneath a kitchen, bathroom or utility sink can come from a loose connection, pipe, trap, seal or fitting. Because the cupboard or surrounding surface can hide the source, it is useful to identify exactly where the water begins before assuming which component has failed.

Slow or Blocked Drains

A drain that becomes progressively slower can indicate a developing blockage. Repeatedly treating the symptom without finding the cause may not solve the underlying problem.

Low Water Pressure

A change in water pressure can have several possible causes. It may relate to a fitting, restriction, supply issue or another part of the plumbing system. If the change is sudden or affects multiple fixtures, further investigation may be appropriate.

Running Toilets

A toilet that continues filling after flushing can waste water and may indicate a problem with the internal mechanism, seal, valve or water level. If the problem persists after a basic adjustment, professional attention may be required.

Maintenance

How Can Plumbing Problems Be Prevented?

Regular attention to plumbing fixtures can help property owners identify problems before they become more disruptive. Check visible pipe connections, taps, toilets, sinks and drains periodically rather than waiting until a leak becomes severe.

Check Visible Connections

Look beneath sinks and around exposed plumbing for signs of moisture, corrosion, movement or deterioration. Do not ignore a connection that repeatedly becomes loose.

Pay Attention to Changes

Changes in water pressure, drainage speed, unusual noises, smells or water usage can provide useful warning signs.

Address Small Leaks

Small leaks should not automatically be considered harmless. Moisture can damage surrounding materials and repeated leaks can become more expensive if left unresolved.

Know Where the Water Shut-Off Is

Property owners should know where the relevant water shut-off point is located. Being able to isolate water quickly can reduce the impact of a sudden leak while assistance is being arranged.

DIY or Professional?

When Should You Call a Plumber?

Simple maintenance checks may be reasonable when the problem is clearly understood and there is no significant risk. However, plumbing systems can involve concealed pipework, water pressure, property damage and connections that need to be installed correctly.

Professional assistance is particularly appropriate when a leak cannot be located, water damage is occurring, a blockage keeps returning, a fitting needs replacement, multiple fixtures are affected or the repair is beyond a straightforward adjustment.

If water is actively escaping and cannot be controlled, prioritise stopping or isolating the water supply where possible and arrange appropriate assistance promptly.

Before You Call

What Information Helps With a Plumbing Job?

A clear description of the problem can make it easier to understand what assistance may be required.

Where practical, note where the problem is occurring, when it started, whether it is constant or intermittent, whether other fixtures are affected and whether there is visible water damage.

A few clear photographs of the affected area can also help show the location and type of fitting involved.

For example, if water is appearing beneath a sink, a photograph showing the sink connections and surrounding area can provide useful context before an assessment.
